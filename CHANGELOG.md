# Changelog

All notable changes to this project will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](http://keepachangelog.com/en/1.0.0/). Per the stability policy (`docs/source/stability.md`), every breaking change lands under a `### Breaking` heading naming the old and new form; a release check confirms the heading exists whenever such a change is in the release.

## [Unreleased]

### Breaking

- One capability namespace: `ModelBackendCapability` and `AnalysisBackendCapability` are replaced by `Capability` (`INTERVENTION`, `LATENT_MODELS`, `GRADIENTS`, `CAPTURE`, `ATTRIBUTION_GRAPH`, `HEAD_ATTRIBUTION`), and `ACTIVATION_INTERVENTION` and `FEATURE_INTERVENTION` merge into `INTERVENTION`, whose `InterventionSupport` record mirrors venngram's `Support` (`ops`, `targets` of `activation`, `units` and `features`, `position_scopes`, `values`, `bases`, `of`). circuit-tracer's `FeatureInterventionSupport` becomes `FeatureTargetSupport`, the detail of its `features` target, and its `feature_intervention_support` property is its `intervention_support`. Components keep existing (a model backend, an analysis backend) and each declares `capabilities`, a record per capability and `requires`: circuit-tracer's analysis backend requires `GRADIENTS` and `LATENT_MODELS`, and `get_module_capabilities` refuses a requirement no other component provides. `ModuleCapabilities` lists each capability's providers (`providers`) and keeps one intervention record per provider, so a target two components reach is kept and `intervention_for(target)` refuses an unqualified request for it (`intervention_for(target, provider=...)` names the path). `PositionScope.POSITIONS` names the explicit positions a feature tuple carries. The spellings `activation_intervention` and `feature_intervention` are refused with `intervention`, the module-level `analysis_capabilities` property is removed, and the conformance report format is `interpretune.conformance.report/2`, its declarations keyed by capability and provider ([#951](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/951))

- Intervention ops: `InterventionMode` is removed and an op is a name validated against venngram's registry (`venngram.ops.OPS`). `InterventionSpec.mode` is `op`, the payload key `mode` is `op`, the shorthand field `intervention_mode` is `intervention_op`, and `required_intervention_modes` is folded into `required_ops`, which names composition ops and tensor ops alike (the loader partitions them; the tensor ops are an op's `required_tensor_ops`); the retired spellings are refused. An executor registry keyed by op name (`interpretune.analysis.backends.ops`: `register_executor`, `executor_for`, `registered_ops`) replaces the branch chain that applied the seven core ops, which it registers from the shared op math together with `replace_with_error`. A backend whose hook computes its edit during the pass declares every registered op (`InterventionSupport.every_registered_op`), including ops registered later, and `InterventionSupport.declared_ops` reads the current set. An op collection's definition may carry an `op_spec` block instead of schemas, making it a tensor op: loading it, under the hub trust opt-in, registers its `OpSpec` with venngram and its executor here with the collection, version and revision as provenance, after checking on a probe that the implementation is shape-preserving, out of place and differentiable in the activation and the operand. The op-definition cache format is 9 ([#952](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/952))

### Added

- Weight-space interventions (`interpretune.tuning.weights`): venngram ops on parameters, materialized on the tensors their addresses select, through the module's tunable view. `apply_weight_patch` applies a venngram weight patch (low-rank, sparse or dense; `add` or `replace`) after checking the tensor's hash against the patch's `base_sha256`; `apply_recipe_weights` applies a recipe's `weights` section (`scale`, `add` and `replace` on a tensor or its units, `reject` and `project` on its output side); `weight_form` materializes a per-neuron recipe at `mlp.out`'s input, at token scope `all`, as the column scales and bias patches it equals, and `check_weight_equivalence` compares the two forms on a batch. Edits are `torch.nn.utils.parametrize` parametrizations by default, so `remove()` restores the base exactly; `reversible=False` writes the edit into the tensor for an export. An edit's operand is `adapter:<name>` when trained, `fitted` when computed in the run, or `instrument` when loaded from a published patch, declared through every module's `tunable_roles()`. The edits are tested against venngram's parameter op vectors, including their stated activation counterparts ([#961](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/961), [#960](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/960))

- `venngram_support(..., weights=True)` declares the parameter targets (`param`, `param_units`), and `recipe_interventions` refuses a recipe whose `weights` section has not been applied (`weights_applied=True` once `apply_recipe_weights` has), rather than translating its stages alone ([#961](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/961))

- A tuning schedule that thaws a quantized or packed tensor (a bitsandbytes 4-bit or 8-bit base weight chosen by name or by address on a composition whose view keeps physical names) is refused when the schedule compiles, naming the phase and the tensor with its dtype, rather than failing nameless at the thaw with torch's floating-point error. finetuning-scheduler is pinned at `76b25047` for the refusal, which its compiler now makes for every view (speediedan/finetuning-scheduler#207). Packed storage no phase thaws stays schedulable around, so a frozen quantized base under trainable adapters still trains ([#758](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/758))

- A recipe's feature `add` and `scale` apply on the analysis-backend surface. A circuit-tracer feature tuple sets a value, so `recipe_feature_interventions` computes $a + d$ and $a \cdot d$ from the feature's clean activation $a$, which the caller passes as `clean_activations` from the backend's new `capture_feature_activations`. The `features` target's detail record, `FeatureTargetSupport`, gains `captures_clean_activations` (circuit-tracer's backend declares it), `venngram_feature_support` declares `add` and `scale` only where it is set, and `check_recipe(..., analysis_backend=True)` refuses them elsewhere. The intervention APIs page states what each op is honoured as, per surface and target ([#965](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/965))

- The venngram integration's value transforms are checked against venngram's own conformance vectors (`venngram.ops.check_transforms`), and now agree with them: the transforms run on a `dose` or `doses` value as well as on evidence (a unit or feature target's dose was refused when it carried a transform), `sign_of` and `sign_aware_scale` still apply only to evidence, as venngram refuses at read, and a statistic may be supplied per `(layer, unit)` member as well as as one value or per layer. A whole-activation op's dose still takes no transform ([#944](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/944))

- The `(lightning, nnsight)` composition is tested for tuning: a phased run under the `FinetuningScheduler` callback changes only what each phase thaws, and the composition runs the tuning conformance cases. An nnsight composition whose `hf_from_pretrained_cfg` sets `lora_cfg` or `bitsandbytesconfig` is now refused at configuration, naming the setting: nnsight builds the model through its own constructor, so neither was ever applied and the run trained the unadapted, unquantized base ([#942](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/942))

- A tuning page for the sparsity chain (`docs/tuning_sparsity_chain.md`): its objective, schedule, gate and enforcement, what three seeds have shown, its limitations (stopping and selection rules on a noisy metric, no step-matched control, a fixed dictionary), and the follow-up program it opens ([#936](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/936); follow-ups #988 to #993)

- The sparsity chain as one phased run: the `speediedan/rte` component's `rte_sparse` variant, on the core loop, with configurations for gemma-3-1b-it (`rte_sparse.gemma3.core`, its published transcoders as `instrument`) and for a CPU (`rte_sparse.gpt2.core`, random transcoders). Training shuffles under the run's seed, as the chain did. The boundary restores the best checkpoint, builds attribution graphs in-process, gates the phase against a sha256-pinned untuned reference, and fills the `fitted` enforcement state for the recovery phase. CI and the conformance seeds pin the republished `speediedan/rte@4db2f48c` ([#899](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/899))

- Attention-aware attribution through the analysis surface (#781). `compute_attribution_graph` takes `freeze_attention` and `freeze_norms` (nnsight backend only; with either `False` each edge is a first-order derivative rather than an exact direct effect), and the regime is recorded on the graph and carried through decomposition, hydration, pruning and storage. `head_loadings` splits a target's incoming edges across the heads of each attention layer, and `head_conductance` credits heads for a measured change between two prompts, split into pattern and value parts. Both are gated by a `HEAD_ATTRIBUTION` capability.

- The public stability policy (`docs/source/stability.md`): through the alpha line the promise is notice, not stability; a small named surface gets one release of notice; everything else is explicitly unstable; persisted artifacts are handled separately; the policy is re-evaluated at 0.1.0. `AGENTS.md`'s backwards-compatibility section now points at it ([#258](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/258))

- venngram is pinned at `c0cfc295`, its main with #80, #81 and #82 (unit sets composed with set algebra, each component map row's measured weight `layout` and a tensor's unit axis, and recipe ops on parameters with weight patches), after `c993c2c3` (#56, #74 and #75: the core `replace_with_error` op that latent models apply, op declarations read from documents, and cooperative roles merged into self-assigning views; a registry reader passes over documents of a newer schema, so a default view keeps working when the registry moves on) ([#887](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/887), [#950](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/950), [#961](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/961))

- The venngram integration applies feature targets and evidence-sourced values (#887, slice 3b). `recipe_interventions` writes `add` and `scale` on a latent model's features at the `hook_sae_acts_post` sub-hook, and the new `recipe_feature_interventions` turns a feature `replace` at explicit positions into circuit-tracer's `(layer, position, feature_idx, value)` tuples. A value can read a column of evidence the caller supplies (`evidence=`), through venngram's `scale`, `abs`, `normalize`, `sign_of` and `sign_aware_scale` transforms, which the tests check against circuit-tracer's own tuple builder; `statistic` multiplies by a statistic the caller supplies (`statistics=`) and a named `normalize` divides by it, as venngram#58's draft defines them. `venngram_support(latent_models=True)` declares feature targets and `venngram_feature_support` declares the analysis-backend path. A set naming both `features` and `units` is now a feature target rather than units of the site's own activation ([#887](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/887))

- The venngram integration writes interventions back as venngram documents (#887, slice 3a). `export_book` writes an intervention payload as an address book carrying it as a treatment: each hook a set, each spec an op in payload order, with operands inline or in a sidecar above venngram's inline limit, and refuses what a treatment cannot carry (a hook naming no layer, an operand with no declared basis, a `scale_factor` on `project` or `patch`, `use_intervention_tensor_as_basis=False`). Every exported treatment applies again through `recipe_interventions`. `support_mapping` now publishes venngram's schema 2 `support_record`, with interpretune's contract as its `steering` section, and `load_recipe` reads a book's treatment wherever it reads a recipe (`treatment=` names one) ([#887](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/887))

- `FTSCoreCallback` builds gates and phase hooks from configuration: `tuning_scheduler_kwargs` passes `gates` and `phase_hooks` to finetuning-scheduler's `TuningScheduler`, which now takes them as constructor arguments, instantiating each `class_path` entry (or the `gate`/`hook` value of an entry that carries registration options). A phase-0 start hook given this way runs at setup, and a gate reaches the tuning scheduler and its module through `GateEvent.tuning_scheduler`. The callback's documented "list of gates" previously raised `TypeError`. finetuning-scheduler is pinned at `17042de9` (its main with #184) ([#899](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/899))

- circuit-tracer compositions tune under `offload='disk'`. Disk offload moves each offloaded module to the meta device and reloads it with `load_state_dict(assign=True)`, which replaced its parameter objects, so `CircuitTracerView` refused the composition. Attribution now runs under torch's `swap_module_params_on_conversion` while `offload='disk'` (`interpretune.adapters.circuit_tracer.tunable_view.offload_keeps_parameters`, set and restored around each attribution), so each new tensor is swapped into the existing parameter: an optimizer built before an attribution steps exactly the thawed tensors after it, and the refusal is lifted ([#855](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/855))

- The venngram integration (`interpretune[venngram]`, `interpretune.analysis.venngram`): a `venngram.ops.Support` declaration derived from a backend's `InterventionSupport` and `CaptureSupport` records rather than restated by hand, checked against a recipe with venngram's own `check_support` plus the operand refusal the record cannot express, and publishable as a `support_record` document that venngram's conformance checker reads without interpretune installed. Recipes load from a venngram document reference (fetched manifest-first and hash-checked, returned with the pinned reference for provenance), a local path or an inline mapping, and a supported recipe's unit-targeted `add` stages translate into the native intervention payload (`recipe_interventions`), scattering per-unit doses at caller-supplied site widths and refusing a unit outside them. The point vocabulary gains `hook_mlp_hidden_in`, the MLP hidden activation addressed at the down projection's input (the same point as `mlp.out.hook_in` and TransformerLens's `mlp.hook_post`), resolving on every bundled architecture ([#887](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/887))

- The venngram integration applies every core venngram op (#887, slice 2). Whole-activation ops at a layered site (`blocks.<layer>.<site>`) map onto the native modes with their operand read inline or from its sidecar beside the recipe: `add` and `replace` with a vector operand (or a broadcast `dose`), `scale`, `project` onto a vector, `reject` with its `fraction`, `patch` on a pair and `clamp` with `min`/`max`, appended in recipe order. Unit-targeted `scale` joins `add`, scattering a per-unit multiplier. The derived declaration names `activation` targets, the layered site spellings and the operand bases it honours (by default interpretune's `jlens_folded` and `jlens_unfolded`), and what has no native mode yet (a layer-free whole-activation op, a subspace or partial `project`, a unit-targeted `replace`, feature targets, evidence-sourced values) is refused ([#887](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/887))

- `InterventionMode.SCALE`: the activation multiplied elementwise by `intervention_tensor * scale_factor` at the spec's scope, with a backend conformance case (scaling by ones is the identity) and an entry in the mode-distinguishability case. `InterventionSupport.every()` now declares it, so the bundled TransformerLens and nnsight backends honour it through the shared intervention path ([#887](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/887))

- Core-loop precision and gradient accumulation (`SessionRunnerCfg.precision` and `accumulate_grad_batches`, honored by `core_train_loop` and `core_test_loop`). `precision` takes Lightning's names: `"bf16-mixed"` autocasts the step functions to bfloat16 around fp32 master weights, `"bf16-true"` autocasts a module whose weights are already bfloat16, and `"32"` never autocasts; the default keeps the module dtype's meaning (autocast exactly when it is bfloat16), and a precision that contradicts the module's dtype is refused. With `accumulate_grad_batches`, each batch's loss is scaled by the reciprocal and the optimizer steps every N batches, flushing a partial window at each epoch's end; step-interval lr schedulers, `on_train_step_end` and `max_steps` count optimizer steps, so a Lightning run with `precision="bf16-mixed"` and `accumulate_grad_batches=2` now has a core-loop equivalent, and an `FTSCoreCallback` schedule still sees only real optimizer steps ([#836](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/836))
- `HFFromPretrainedConfig.meta_init` builds a `from_pretrained` model on the meta device in every process, so a data-parallel placement shards it before it holds any weights and then loads it from a copy rank 0 keeps on the CPU, with buffers broadcast too: no accelerator holds the whole model. A copy that does not match the model (a missing or extra key, a different shape) is refused on every rank, naming the keys. Data-parallel placements register the calls a composition makes outside its forward as FSDP2 forward methods (`fsdp_direct_calls`): nnsight's direct call of the Hugging Face model, and circuit-tracer's transcoder `encode`, `decode`, skip and activation-function calls. A circuit-tracer composition sharded on two ranks gives the logits, nnsight traces and transcoder activations of one process, and a plan that leaves a called module to the root unit is refused naming the `class:` keys to add. `meta_init` takes `lora_cfg`: rank 0's copy gets the same LoRA adapters, so their initial values load on every rank with the base weights (`meta_init_load: dcp` refuses LoRA, since checkpoint files hold no adapters). `HFFromPretrainedConfig.meta_init_load: dcp` loads a `meta_init` model's shards on every rank straight from the checkpoint's safetensors files with distributed checkpointing, so no host holds the whole model either; non-persistent buffers are computed at build time and kept, keys are renamed as `from_pretrained` renames them, and a key no model key matches, a parameter no key supplies, or a checkpoint that needs weight converters is refused. A circuit-tracer composition over nnsight builds on the meta device under `meta_init` (Hugging Face and transcoder parameters alike, transcoders loaded eagerly) and loads from rank 0's CPU copy into the Hugging Face module nnsight wraps, which a module names with `meta_load_target()`; on two ranks it gives the logits, nnsight trace and transcoder activations of the copy in one process. Compositions whose model init does not build on the meta device (TransformerLens bridges, plain nnsight models, `defer_model_init`) refuse `meta_init` at construction instead of ignoring it. The `interpretune` CLI's documented `torchrun` launch is now tested end to end, with and without `meta_init` ([#882](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/882))
- The core loop's data-parallel placements take finetuning-scheduler's FSDP2 plan: `SessionRunnerCfg.fsdp_plan` (module names, regexes and `class:` keys mapped to `fully_shard` keyword arguments, with the `act_ckpt` and `cpu_offload_policy` aliases) and `fsdp_default_kwargs`, applied with `shard_model` deepest first whatever the plan's key order. `shard_units` is retired: `shard_units: [X]` becomes `fsdp_plan: {class:X: {}}`, which also matches subclasses of `X`. Phased tuning with `FTSCoreCallback` under `fsdp2` is now tested on two ranks ([#852](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/852))
- A run configuration loads the Hub adapter components its session needs (`hub_adapters: [<org>/<repo>@<commit>]`, or `--hub_adapters`), so a hub adapter's composition runs from YAML alone on both CLIs. Each entry must be pinned to a commit; the trust gate is checked before anything is downloaded, an uncached revision is pulled at that revision, and the entries load before the arguments are parsed so `it_hub_adapters.*` class paths resolve. `interpretune.hub.loaded_hub_adapters()` records each component's executed revision, carried in a module's hyperparameters as `hub_adapters`, and an unregistered adapter in an adapter context names the cached component that declares it ([#872](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/872))

- `CircuitTracerConfig.tune_base` lets a circuit-tracer composition tune its base model as `base` under transcoders that stay `instrument` unless promoted; base tensors are not promotable. Attribution now runs with the replacement model frozen, since circuit-tracer attributes with `backward()`: a thawed embedding failed it outright and other thawed base parameters accumulated attribution gradients into `.grad`. Attributing with thawed base parameters warns, suggesting re-fitting the transcoders when their fit has degraded ([#813](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/813))
- circuit-tracer compositions are tunable through their transcoders (`interpretune.adapters.circuit_tracer.tunable_view.CircuitTracerView`), replacing the refusal they shipped with: every transcoder tensor starts as `instrument` and is promotable to `latent:transcoder`, base-model parameters are refused, and `offload='disk'` refuses the composition. `CircuitTracerConfig.lazy_delta_rank` gives lazily loaded transcoder weights a trainable low-rank correction that every circuit-tracer read (whole-matrix and decoder-row) applies, so the memory-saving lazy layouts stay tunable. The interp-engine replacement model is no longer registered twice on the module, and the nnsight backend's transcoders are registered once. `interpretune.adapters.circuit_tracer.drift.TranscoderFitMeter` measures per-layer transcoder drift: fraction of variance unexplained, $L_0$, and dead and dense feature counts ([#787](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/787), [#709](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/709))
- Data-parallel placement for the core training and test loops (`SessionRunnerCfg.placement`: `single`, `ddp` or `fsdp2`, launched with `torchrun`; `interpretune.runners.distributed`). Both data-parallel placements apply FSDP2's `fully_shard` in place (`ddp` over a replicate mesh), so no wrapper stands between callers and `module.model`. The runner builds the session one rank at a time with data prepared once per host, broadcasts rank 0's weights, shards (`fsdp2` by the classes `shard_units` names, deepest first), rebuilds each loader over a `DistributedSampler` and averages logged metrics across ranks. Mismatched launches, layer splits, shared accelerators, non-contiguous parameters and loaders that cannot be rebuilt faithfully are refused. Two-rank CPU runs of both placements match one process training on their combined batch, step for step. `it_init` gains `prepare_data` and `configure_optimizers` switches, and `it_configure_optimizers` builds the optimizers on its own ([#20](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/20), [#803](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/803))
- Memory-profiling delta pairs for the analysis adapters (`tests/profiling_delta`): `sae_lens_train`/`sae_lens_test` against SAELens's own `SAETransformerBridge` with the same SAE attached through `add_sae`, `nnsight_test` against a bare nnsight `LanguageModel`, and `circuit_tracer_test` against a bare circuit-tracer `ReplacementModel` attributing the same prompt. Their bounds, and those of `tl_train`/`tl_test`, are frozen on both vendors ([#825](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/825), [#823](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/823))

- Latent models for tuning (`interpretune.tuning.latents`): any `nn.Module` with `encode` and `decode` (an SAE, a transcoder, a block-sparse featurizer) can be spliced in at a site (`LatentSite`: the module it reads, input or output, the module whose output it rewrites, and the op applied there with the reconstruction as its operand: venngram's `replace`, or its straight-through substitution `replace_with_error`; with no op the latents are only captured) and registered on the module as `latent_models.<name>` with `attach_latent_model`. Its tensors take venngram's role `latent:<name>`, or `instrument` when attached frozen, until finetuning-scheduler's `promote` key makes them latent. Every interpretune module declares these roles in its own `tunable_roles()`, which its venngram view reads. A circuit-tracer composition refuses the splice for now, since its view assigns roles itself ([#953](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/953) tracks lifting it) ([#812](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/812), [#950](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/950))

- Trainable interventions for tuning (`interpretune.tuning.interventions`): an op from venngram's registry applied at a site with a trainable operand (`OpIntervention`; `add` and `scale` for now, through the op math every model backend shares, made out of place as `apply_op`). A steering vector is `add` with a learned vector (`OpIntervention("add", d_model)`), and an operand's `shape` may be a tuple (a per-head gain); each operand starts where its op is the identity. One is spliced in at a module path with `attach_intervention` and registered on the module as `interventions.<name>`. Its tensors take venngram's role `intervention:<name>` (`role:intervention` selects every intervention), or `instrument` for a precomputed one attached frozen. The docs' schedules run as stages with finetuning-scheduler's `refreeze` and `restore` keys: train a vector, then hold it fixed while blocks tune. finetuning-scheduler is pinned at `db0305d5` (its main with those keys) ([#812](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/812), [#950](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/950))

- Trainable interventions and latent models attach to circuit-tracer compositions where nothing conflicts: the module's declared roles are merged into the transcoders' view through venngram's cooperative-role rule (a conflicting declaration is refused), a site must be a module of the model circuit-tracer runs (the replacement model on TransformerLens, the HF model it wraps on nnsight), and attribution runs with the module's own thawed tensors frozen too, so it attributes the intervened model without reaching their gradients, warning with the fit-measurement suggestion whenever a tensor outside the transcoders is thawed ([#953](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/953))

- Interventions declared in a module's configuration (`ITConfig.tuning_interventions`, a list of `InterventionCfg` (a name, a site and an op) or the mappings a YAML file gives) are attached once the model is built and before its view, on its site's device, so a steered run needs no module code on any backend. A configured intervention is always tunable: a later stage holds it fixed with `refreeze` ([#812](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/812))

- Memory-profiling deltas (`tests/profiling_delta`): each sampled composition runs beside a matched upstream arm (plain PyTorch and Hugging Face, a vanilla `LightningModule`, or the same `TuningScheduler` in a plain loop), one subprocess per arm, and the extended profiling tier gates on the interpretune-minus-upstream delta in peak and retained bytes, per-phase leak slope and phase-transition step. Bounds are frozen per vendor from NVIDIA and AMD measurements (`freeze.py`); planted leak and overhead controls prove the gate can fail; phased runs also assert exact structural invariants; release and tag builds run it report-only ([#823](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/823), [#811](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/811))

- Phase reports for the GPU pipeline, ported from finetuning-scheduler: each GPU phase's manifest is collected on the CPU before the GPU work, every pytest process the phase starts records its outcomes (`tests/gpu_ci/phase_plugin.py`), and a report per phase counts them against the manifest, collected beside passed, skipped and failed, under the vendor the agent reported (`tests/gpu_ci/phase_report.py`). A failing phase fails the job, and so does an empty one on a full run; the manifests, records and reports are published as the job's phase evidence. A release pipeline (`.azure-pipelines/gpu-release.yml`) runs one vendor-demanded leg per vendor and checks both legs' evidence (with the shared checker below): one full, clean run per vendor, with a reviewed reason for every skip (`tests/gpu_ci/reviewed_exclusions.json`) ([#703](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/703))

- Phase-specific collection manifests and a ROCm failure-triage classifier for the CUDA-marked and standalone test phases (`tests/gpu_ci/phase_manifest.py`), with a committed measured manifest and build-day commands; ROCm execution remains pending AMD hardware ([#794](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/794))

- ROCm triage buckets measured on the first dual-vendor parity runs: `device-fault` (device-side asserts, launch failures, HSA exceptions; not ROCm evidence by itself), `resource-calibration` and `numerics-tolerance`, plus `--classify-junit`, which reads a phase's JUnit file in execution order and attributes a sticky device-context cascade to its first fault (`device-fault-cascade`). The circuit-tracer conformance commuting control now reports its measured difference ([#794](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/794))

### Changed

- The `rte_sparse` variant's recovery re-thaws in two steps, as the sparsity chain did (#700), with finetuning-scheduler's per-phase `refreeze` key: phase 1 refreezes the earlier half of the sparsity phase's blocks (14-21 on gemma-3-1b-it, 9 and 10 on GPT-2) and trains the rest with the simplified graph enforced, and phase 2 thaws them again under the same enforcement. Phase 1 restores the sparsity phase's best (`restore: best`) and phase 2 the first step's own best (`restore: best:1`, finetuning-scheduler#196), and the plateau transition judges each stage against its own best (`reset_best_on_phase`), so the second step always continues from the first, as the chain's order requires, even where the sparsity phase scored better on `val_task`; each recovery phase keeps its fresh optimizer. A core-loop FTS run resumes this way: `FTSCoreCallback`'s state is finetuning-scheduler's `TuningScheduler.resume_state()`, which marks the resume point with its checkpoint policy, and loading it rebuilds the optimizer phase by phase before the loop loads the optimizer's state (finetuning-scheduler pinned at `87e1d6d3`, its #144) ([#809](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/809))

### Fixed

- Analysis injection patches a module whose docstring carries a string prefix (`r"""`, as circuit-tracer's `head_conductance.py` opens). The patcher read such a docstring as the first statement and put its `HOOK_REGISTRY` import above the module's `from __future__` import, so the patched module failed to compile with a `SyntaxError` as soon as a hook targeted that file.
- `recipe_interventions` refuses an op whose target set addresses a gradient (`of: grad`) instead of translating it into an op on the activation value, which applied a different treatment silently when `require_supported` was skipped; `check_recipe` lists it too. `venngram_support` now carries the record's `of` into the declaration, so venngram's own check refuses such a recipe for every backend, since each declares `value` only ([#938](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/938))

- `FTSCoreCallback` resolves a `class_path` naming a Hub component's entrypoint (`rte_boolq.ChainCalibrate`, say) for its gates, hooks and other `tuning_scheduler_kwargs` objects, as session configurations do; it raised `ModuleNotFoundError`. Configuration loading also passes a list's scalars through `instantiate_nested`, which raised `TypeError` on `reg_layers: [17, 18]` ([#899](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/899))

- Moving a loaded circuit-tracer replacement model moves its attribution device too. It was recorded at load, so `safe_clean_cuda` on a CPU-loaded model sent activations to the CPU while the transcoders reading them were on the GPU, which failed two Gemma 3 session parity tests with a device error (#915).

- circuit-tracer token nodes carry the embedding the model adds rather than the raw embedding row. Under transformers 5, Gemma 2 and Gemma 3 scale by $\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{model}}}$ inside their embedding module, so every token node's edges were too small by that factor (33.9 times on gemma-3-1b, 48 times on gemma-2-2b), which also shifted influence, pruning and the graph scores built on them. - `offload='disk'` runs on Windows: circuit-tracer wrote each offload file while still holding it open, and a reload onto the CPU left it memory-mapped when deleting it, both of which Windows refuses ([#909](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/909))
- A data-parallel placement gathers a unit's parameters for its registered direct calls only at the outermost one: a registered method calling another on the same unit (circuit-tracer's `decode_sparse` calls `_get_decoder_vectors`) resharded the unit when the inner call returned, so the outer read a sharded parameter and attribution's setup failed with `aten.add.Tensor got mixed torch.Tensor and DTensor` ([#882](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/882))
- `hf_from_pretrained_cfg.lora_cfg` without `bitsandbytesconfig` applies LoRA to the unquantized model. It was applied only alongside a bitsandbytes configuration and otherwise ignored without a warning ([#884](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/884))

The `rte` component (`speediedan/rte`) applies `shuffle` and `drop_last` to the training split only, so evaluation scores every example in order, and its `rte_ft.*.core` configurations drop a `drop_last: true` that never took effect, which keeps the fine-tuning benchmark's runs as measured. CI and the conformance seeds pin the republished `speediedan/rte@14d388e1` ([#858](https://github.com/speediedan/interpretune/issues/858))
